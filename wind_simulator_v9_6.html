It uses a simplified 2D Gauss-Seidel pressure solver and particle-based flow visualisation — it does not implement a full Navier-Stokes CFD solver, turbulence models (k-ε, k-ω, LES), or three-dimensional flow analysis. How Wind Flow is Calculated
Wind Flow Simulation uses a real-time 2D pressure-velocity field solved on a uniform Cartesian grid. The simulation runs entirely in the browser using JavaScript canvas rendering at interactive frame rates.
Pressure Solver
→Gauss-Seidel iterative relaxation solves for the pressure field each frame. The solver runs forward and backward sweeps to propagate pressure from obstacle faces into the surrounding flow domain.
→Source injection on windward obstacle faces generates positive pressure (compression); leeward faces generate negative pressure (suction/wake).
→Neumann boundary conditions on all four canvas edges allow air to pass through freely — simulating an open exterior domain with no artificial inlet/outlet pressure effects.
→Pressure decay per frame prevents unbounded accumulation and allows the field to respond dynamically to geometry changes.
Velocity Field
→Ambient body force applies a uniform baseline wind velocity in the user-defined direction at every free grid cell, representing the undisturbed far-field wind.
→Pressure gradient deflection modifies the velocity at each cell by subtracting the local ∇P (pressure gradient), steering flow around high-pressure zones.
→Porous obstacles (trees) apply a permeability factor that partially reduces wind speed through the element, simulating vegetation resistance.
Particle Visualisation
→Massless tracer particles are advected through the velocity field using bilinear interpolation. They are not physically accurate fluid parcels — they are visualisation streamers that approximate flow paths.
→Particle colour maps to local speed (green → amber → red). ASHRAE comfort thresholds are applied to zone-averaged speeds for preliminary occupant comfort assessment.
Grid & Scale
→The simulation grid uses a uniform cell size (High: 8 px/cell, Low: 16 px/cell). Real-world scale is set at project launch: 0.20 m/cell (Small), 0.50 m/cell (Medium), or 2.00 m/cell (Large).
Known Limitations
The following are inherent constraints of the current simulation approach. They are important to understand when interpreting results.
✕2D only. All flow is simulated in a single horizontal plane. Vertical airflow, stack effect, buoyancy, and three-dimensional recirculation zones are not captured.
✕No turbulence modelling. The solver does not implement any turbulence closure model (k-ε, k-ω, LES, DNS). Turbulent fluctuations are approximated by a random noise term only.
✕Incompressible, steady-state approximation. The pressure field is solved quasi-statically each frame. True transient flow phenomena, pressure waves, and compressibility effects are not modelled.
✕No thermal effects. Temperature gradients, solar gain, buoyancy-driven convection, and thermal plumes are not included.
✕Simplified wall interaction. Walls are treated as binary obstacles on a discrete grid. Curved surfaces are approximated as stepped cells. Boundary layer development along wall surfaces is not resolved.
✕Grid resolution limits accuracy. At the default cell size, small openings, narrow gaps, or fine geometry may not be resolved accurately. Results improve with higher resolution settings.
✕Particle visualisation ≠ streamlines. Particles are advected tracers, not true streamlines or pathlines. They provide a qualitative impression of flow direction and relative speed.
✕ASHRAE comfort values are indicative only. Zone-averaged speeds use simplified polygon sampling. They are not equivalent to validated CFD ASHRAE 55 or ISO 7730 assessments.
✕Not validated against experimental data. The solver has not been benchmarked against wind tunnel tests or validated CFD cases. It is intended for design intuition, not engineering certification.
